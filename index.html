How sure are we about an average?

Every time you measure something, you get a slightly different number by chance. Your average of 5 trials is your best guess at the truth, but it is not exact. This page helps you see how much to trust it, using your own toothpick data.

The experiment, in one line

Break 20 toothpicks one at a time, timing yourself in seconds — do this 5 times with your dominant hand and 5 times with your non-dominant hand. Those 10 numbers are the "trial data" this whole page uses.

Three ideas, in plain language

You will use three numbers throughout this activity. Here is what each one is really asking.

SPREAD (SD)

How different your 5 trials were from each other. Big spread means your times bounced around a lot.

PRECISION (SEM)

How much you would expect your average to change if you ran the whole experiment again. More trials shrinks this number.

CONFIDENCE INTERVAL

A range around your average that probably contains the real, true average — not just the one you happened to measure.

Concept A — Why square the deviations?

Uses your dominant-hand trials from Part 1, above. Deviations always sum to zero — that is why we square them before adding.

Concept B — Why divide by n − 1?

Uses your non-dominant-hand trials from Part 1, above. Once you know the mean and all but one value, the last one is locked in — it has no freedom left.
